## 1. How to read this policy

### 1.1 The agreement
This wording, your declarations page, and any endorsements listed on it form the whole agreement
between you and us. Where the declarations page and this wording disagree, the declarations page
controls. Words in bold on your declarations page have the meaning given in clause 1.2.

### 1.2 Words we use
"You" means the named insured on the declarations page and residents of their household who are
relatives or are under 21 and in their care. "We" means the company issuing this policy. "The
residence" means the dwelling at the address shown on the declarations page. "Sudden and
accidental" means an event that begins at an identifiable point in time and is neither expected
nor intended by you. "Actual cash value" means replacement cost less depreciation for age and
condition.

## 2. What we cover

### 2.1 The dwelling
We cover sudden and accidental direct physical loss to the residence and to structures attached to
it, up to the dwelling limit shown on your declarations page. Covered causes include fire, lightning,
windstorm, hail, falling objects, the weight of ice or snow, and the sudden and accidental discharge
of water from a plumbing, heating, or air conditioning system inside the residence. We do not cover
losses excluded in part 3.

### 2.2 Detached structures
We cover detached garages, fences, sheds, and similar structures on the residence premises for the
same causes of loss as the dwelling, up to ten percent of the dwelling limit. This amount is in
addition to the dwelling limit. Structures used for business are not covered under this clause.

### 2.3 Personal property
We cover your household goods and personal belongings anywhere in the world, up to the personal
property limit shown on your declarations page. Jewelry, watches, and furs are covered for theft up
to a sublimit of $2,500 for any one loss unless a higher amount is scheduled on the declarations
page. Money, securities, and business property carry their own lower sublimits shown on your
declarations page.

### 2.4 Loss of use
If a covered loss makes the residence unfit to live in, we pay the reasonable increase in your
living expenses for the shortest time needed to repair or replace it, up to twenty percent of the
dwelling limit. We pay only the amount by which your costs exceed what you normally spend. Keep your
receipts: we settle this from documented costs, not estimates.

### 2.5 Water backup and sump overflow endorsement
Where the declarations page lists a water backup endorsement, we cover direct physical loss caused
by water that backs up through sewers or drains, or that overflows from a sump, sump pump, or
related equipment, up to the endorsement limit. This cover applies whether or not the backup or
overflow was caused by a mechanical breakdown of the sump pump. It does not extend to surface water
or flood, which remain excluded by clause 3.1.

### 2.6 Reasonable emergency measures
We pay the reasonable cost of emergency measures you take to protect covered property from further
damage after a covered loss, such as water extraction, drying equipment, or boarding up an opening.
Keep the invoices. This payment does not increase the limits that apply to the loss itself.

## 3. What we do not cover

### 3.1 Flood and surface water
We do not cover loss caused by flood, surface water, waves, tidal water, the overflow of a body of
water, or spray from any of these, whether or not driven by wind. Water that enters the residence
at or below ground level from outside the building is excluded by this clause. Cover for backup
through sewers and drains, and for sump overflow, is given only by clause 2.5.

### 3.2 Gradual damage, wear, and maintenance
We do not cover loss caused by wear and tear, deterioration, rust, corrosion, settling, or by
continuous or repeated seepage or leakage of water over a period of more than fourteen days. Damage
that develops slowly is a maintenance cost, not a sudden and accidental loss. The resulting damage
from a specific pipe that bursts suddenly remains covered under clause 2.1.

### 3.3 Mold, fungus, and rot
We do not cover loss caused by mold, fungus, wet or dry rot, or bacteria, or the cost of testing for
or remediating them, except where they result directly from a covered water loss that you reported
and mitigated as required by part 5. Where that exception applies, we pay for remediation up to
$10,000 for any one loss.

### 3.4 Neglect and failure to mitigate
We do not cover damage that results from your failure to take reasonable steps to protect the
property after a loss, or from neglecting to maintain the property before one. If part of the
damage would have been avoided by prompt action, we pay only the part that would have occurred
anyway.

### 3.5 Earth movement and vacancy
We do not cover loss caused by earthquake, landslide, sinkhole collapse, or other earth movement.
We do not cover vandalism, malicious mischief, or water damage where the residence has been vacant
for more than sixty consecutive days before the loss.

### 3.6 Business and intentional acts
We do not cover property used primarily for a business conducted at the residence, or any loss you
cause on purpose. Loss caused intentionally by any insured voids cover for that loss for every
insured under this policy.

## 4. Your deductible

### 4.1 How the deductible applies
We pay the amount of a covered loss that exceeds the deductible shown on your declarations page. The
deductible applies once to each loss, not to each damaged item. Where two deductibles could apply to
the same loss, we apply the higher one only.

### 4.2 Windstorm and hail deductible
Where the declarations page shows a separate windstorm or hail deductible, that amount applies
instead of the all perils deductible to any loss caused by wind, hail, or a named storm, including
damage from rain or debris entering through an opening the storm created.

## 5. What you must do after a loss

### 5.1 Tell us promptly
You must give us notice of a loss as soon as reasonably possible, and in any event within ninety
days of the date of loss. Where notice is later than that, we may reduce or decline payment to the
extent the delay has prejudiced our ability to investigate. Notice by phone to our claims line is
enough to meet this clause.

### 5.2 Protect the property from further damage
You must take reasonable steps to protect covered property from further damage and keep a record of
what you spend doing so. Do not begin permanent repairs before we have had a reasonable opportunity
to inspect, unless the repair is needed to make the residence safe or to stop ongoing damage.

### 5.3 Show us what happened
You must send us, within a reasonable time of our request, photographs of the damaged areas taken
before cleanup where you have them, a repair estimate or contractor assessment, invoices for
emergency measures and mitigation work, and proof of ownership for items you claim. Where the loss
involves theft or vandalism, you must also report it to the police and give us the report number.

### 5.4 Cooperate with our investigation
You must cooperate with us, answer reasonable questions about the loss, and let us inspect the
property and the damaged items before they are discarded. Keep damaged items where it is safe to do
so until we have inspected them or told you we do not need to.

### 5.5 Fraud and misrepresentation
This policy is void as to any loss where you or anyone acting for you has intentionally concealed or
misrepresented a material fact, made a false statement about the loss, or presented an inflated or
invented claim.

## 6. How we settle a claim

### 6.1 Replacement cost and actual cash value
We settle covered losses to the dwelling on a replacement cost basis where you actually repair or
replace the damaged property. Until you do, we pay the actual cash value. Personal property is
settled at actual cash value unless the declarations page shows replacement cost cover for
contents.

### 6.2 When we pay
We pay within thirty days of agreeing the amount of the loss with you. Where part of a claim is
agreed and part is still being reviewed, we pay the agreed part without waiting for the rest.

### 6.3 If we disagree on the amount
If you and we disagree on the amount of a loss, either of us may demand an appraisal. Each side
chooses a competent appraiser, the two appraisers choose an umpire, and an agreement by any two of
the three sets the amount. Each side pays its own appraiser and shares the umpire's costs equally.
An appraisal decides the amount of a loss, never whether the loss is covered.
