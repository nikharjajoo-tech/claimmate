## 1. How to read this policy

### 1.1 The agreement
This wording, your declarations page, and any endorsements listed on it form the whole agreement
between you and us. This policy covers your belongings and your additional living costs. It does not
cover the building you live in, which is your landlord's responsibility to insure.

### 1.2 Words we use
"You" means the named insured on the declarations page and relatives living in the same unit. "The
unit" means the rented residence at the address shown on the declarations page. "Actual cash value"
means replacement cost less depreciation for age and condition. "Sudden and accidental" means an
event that begins at an identifiable point in time and is neither expected nor intended by you.

## 2. What we cover

### 2.1 Your personal property
We cover your household goods and personal belongings against sudden and accidental direct physical
loss, up to the personal property limit shown on your declarations page. Covered causes include
fire, smoke, theft, vandalism, windstorm, falling objects, the weight of ice or snow, and the sudden
and accidental discharge of water from plumbing or appliances inside the building.

### 2.2 Property away from the unit
We cover your belongings anywhere in the world, including in a vehicle, a hotel room, or a
workplace. For theft occurring away from the unit, we pay up to ten percent of your personal
property limit for any one loss. Property you have lent, rented out, or left in the care of someone
who is not an insured is covered only for fire and theft.

### 2.3 Loss of use
If a covered loss makes the unit unfit to live in, we pay the reasonable increase in your living
expenses, including temporary accommodation, up to the loss of use limit shown on your declarations
page. We pay only the amount by which your costs exceed what you normally spend, for the shortest
time reasonably needed to repair the unit or for you to settle elsewhere.

### 2.4 Sublimits for particular items
Unless a higher amount is scheduled on your declarations page, we pay no more than $1,500 for
jewelry, watches, and furs taken by theft; $2,000 for electronics and computing equipment in any one
loss; $250 for money, bank notes, and gift cards; and $1,000 for bicycles and their accessories.
These sublimits are part of, not in addition to, your personal property limit.

### 2.5 Damage you cause to the unit
We cover sudden and accidental damage you cause to the unit itself, such as a kitchen fire or water
escaping from an appliance you own, up to $5,000 for any one loss. This clause does not cover normal
wear, marks, or damage your landlord would ordinarily deduct from a security deposit.

## 3. What we do not cover

### 3.1 Flood and surface water
We do not cover loss caused by flood, surface water, or the overflow of a body of water, whether or
not driven by wind. Water that enters the unit at or below ground level from outside the building is
excluded by this clause.

### 3.2 Gradual damage, wear, and pests
We do not cover wear and tear, deterioration, mechanical breakdown, or damage caused by insects,
vermin, or domestic animals. We do not cover continuous or repeated seepage of water over a period
of more than fourteen days.

### 3.3 Mysterious disappearance
We do not cover property that is simply missing, where there is no evidence of theft and you cannot
say when or where it was lost. A theft claim needs a place, an approximate time, and a police report
under clause 5.3.

### 3.4 Business property and vehicles
We do not cover property held for sale or used primarily in a business, or motor vehicles and their
parts, other than portable electronics you also use for work.

### 3.5 Mold and intentional acts
We do not cover mold, fungus, or rot, or the cost of testing for or remediating them, except where
they result directly from a covered water loss you reported and mitigated as required by part 5, in
which case we pay up to $5,000. We do not cover any loss you cause on purpose.

## 4. Your deductible

### 4.1 How the deductible applies
We pay the amount of a covered loss that exceeds the deductible shown on your declarations page. The
deductible applies once to each loss, not to each item. Where a sublimit under clause 2.4 applies,
we apply the sublimit first and the deductible second.

## 5. What you must do after a loss

### 5.1 Tell us promptly
You must give us notice of a loss as soon as reasonably possible, and in any event within ninety
days of the date of loss. Where notice is later than that, we may reduce or decline payment to the
extent the delay has prejudiced our ability to investigate.

### 5.2 Protect what is left
You must take reasonable steps to protect undamaged property from further loss, and keep a record of
what you spend doing so. Where a door or window has been forced, securing the unit is your
responsibility until your landlord repairs it.

### 5.3 Report theft to the police
For any claim involving theft, burglary, or vandalism, you must report the incident to the police
and give us the report or case number. We cannot settle a theft claim without it.

### 5.4 Show us what you owned
You must send us, within a reasonable time of our request, a list of the items claimed with their
approximate age and value, and proof of ownership where you have it: receipts, bank or card
statements, serial numbers, photographs, or the original packaging. Where you have no proof for an
item, tell us so rather than leaving it out; we will consider other evidence.

### 5.5 Fraud and misrepresentation
This policy is void as to any loss where you or anyone acting for you has intentionally concealed or
misrepresented a material fact, made a false statement about the loss, or presented an inflated or
invented claim.

## 6. How we settle a claim

### 6.1 Actual cash value
We settle personal property claims at actual cash value unless the declarations page shows
replacement cost cover for contents. Where replacement cost cover applies, we pay the actual cash
value first and the balance once you have actually replaced the item and sent us the receipt.

### 6.2 When we pay
We pay within thirty days of agreeing the amount of the loss with you. Where part of a claim is
agreed and part is still being reviewed, we pay the agreed part without waiting for the rest.

### 6.3 Recovered property
If property is recovered after we have paid for it, you may keep the property and return our payment
or keep the payment and give the property to us. Tell us promptly if the police recover any item you
have claimed for.

### 6.4 If we disagree on the amount
If you and we disagree on the amount of a loss, either of us may demand an appraisal. Each side
chooses a competent appraiser, the two appraisers choose an umpire, and an agreement by any two of
the three sets the amount. An appraisal decides the amount of a loss, never whether the loss is
covered.
